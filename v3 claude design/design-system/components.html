Logo

The Venzza mark is a geometric V inside a perfect circle, with a diamond at its lower convergence — the point where people, ideas, brands, and creators meet. Its idea is Connection · Alignment · Value. Everything here renders from the exact source SVGs — the single source of truth. Never redraw, re-space, or approximate the mark; download the file you need.

Construction

The symbol lives inside a true circle (a 600 × 600 space). The V sits at the optical center and never touches the circle edge; the diamond marks its lower convergence. Proportions are fixed — use the files, don’t rebuild them.

  • Circle: a perfect 600 × 600 field — geometric, not optical.
  • V: occupies the optical center, balanced, never touching the edge.
  • Diamond: the convergence point — also the unit for clear space.
Clear space

Keep clear space of 1 diamond unit (the width of the center diamond) on all four sides. Nothing — type, imagery, or edges — enters this zone.

1× diamond

Color

The mark lives in black or white. A single approved brand color — Venzza electric lime — may be used in primary-action contexts only. No other colors. For photography or color fields, use the knockout symbol, which reveals the background through the V.

Knockout on color

The knockout symbol punches the V clean through the circle, so the background shows within the mark. Use it over photography or a solid brand color — never recolor the solid symbol.

Accessibility · ButtonAccessibility
  • Use real <button> elements — activatable with both Enter and Space, and reachable by keyboard.
  • Icon-only buttons carry an aria-label; the visible label and its toast/result stay consistent (“Publish” → “Published”).
  • Focus is always visible via the brand ring; never remove the outline without a replacement.
  • Loading buttons set aria-busy="true" and block repeat clicks until the action resolves.
  • Every button — including small — meets a 44px hit target.
Do / Don't
Do
  • Keep one primary action per view.
  • Label with a verb + object (“Save changes”).
  • Style destructive actions distinctly.
  • Show a loading state for async actions.
Don't
  • Stack multiple primaries competing for attention.
  • Use vague labels like “OK” or “Submit”.
  • Signal “destructive” with color alone.
  • Disable a button without explaining why.

Anatomy · InputAnatomy
We only use this for receipts.
  1. Label — always visible; names what the field holds.

Accessibility · InputAccessibility
  • Every field has a persistent <label> tied by for/id — placeholders never stand in for labels.
  • Errors set aria-invalid="true" and link the message with aria-describedby; status is signalled by icon + text, never color alone.
  • Focus is always visible via :focus-visible (the brand ring); focus order follows reading order.
  • Interactive controls meet a 44px hit target; the trailing reveal/clear buttons are real, keyboard-reachable buttons.
  • Live validation announces through an aria-live="polite" region so screen readers hear it.
Do / Don't
Do
  • Keep labels visible above the field.
  • Show errors inline, next to the field, with an icon.
  • Use placeholders for format examples (“you@venzza.app”).
  • Reserve the trailing slot for one clear action.
Don't
  • Use the placeholder as the only label.
  • Signal errors with red border alone.
  • Disable a submit without explaining why.
  • Crowd the field with multiple trailing icons.

Accessibility · OverlayAccessibility
  • Dialogs use role="dialog" (or alertdialog) with aria-modal="true" and an aria-labelledby title.
  • Focus moves into the dialog on open, is trapped with Tab / Shift-Tab, and returns to the trigger on close.
  • Every overlay closes with Esc; modals also close on scrim click, and the primary action sits last.
  • Tooltips appear on focus as well as hover, so keyboard users get them too — and never hold the only copy of critical info.
  • Coach marks are dismissible (Skip) and never block the underlying task permanently.
Do / Don't
Do
  • Use a modal for a single, focused task.
  • Lead destructive confirms with the danger icon + red action.
  • Keep tooltips to a few words.
  • Return focus to the trigger on close.
Don't
  • Stack modals on top of modals.
  • Put a form’s only instructions in a tooltip.
  • Trap users with no visible way to close.
  • Use a dialog where inline UI would do.

Accessibility · DataAccessibility
  • The table is a real <table> with header cells; sort controls are buttons, and the select-all checkbox reflects an indeterminate state when only some rows are chosen.
  • Every checkbox has an accessible label; selected rows are conveyed by background and the checked control, not colour alone.
  • Interactive cards are focusable (tabindex="0") with a visible focus ring and behave like a button.
  • Status is always a badge with text; trends pair an arrow icon with a signed value.
  • Avatars carry initials as text; presence dots are backed by a label wherever they convey meaning.
Do / Don't
Do
  • Right-align numeric columns and keep units consistent.
  • Show selection count and bulk actions when rows are selectable.
  • Use stat tiles for single, glanceable numbers.
  • Give tables a clear empty and loading state.
Don't
  • Pack a card with unrelated actions.
  • Sort by a column with no meaningful order.
  • Rely on row colour alone to signal state.

Product and Brand share one identity — the mark, the electric lime, the neutral spine, the name and voice. They diverge only in how expressively that identity is applied. Product optimizes for clarity and use; Brand optimizes for attention and feeling.

Brand · Expression

Motion

Where product motion is quiet feedback, brand motion is confident and kinetic — entrances that reveal, geometry that assembles, hover states with a little swagger. Always purposeful, never busy. Every effect below is live and respects prefers-reduced-motion.

Principles
01
Arrive, don’t appear
Headlines and blocks reveal on scroll — a short, eased rise + fade. One beat, then still.
02
Geometry with meaning
When things move, they perform the idea: points connect, the diamond resolves. Motion tells the brand story.
03
Confident, not frantic
Fewer, larger moves. 300–700ms, custom easing. Hover adds energy, not noise.

Marketing tokens WIP

Below: what’s new, what conflicts, and what I recommend promoting.

Hard rule carried over: no brand component references an accent hex or rgba() accent tint directly — everything routes through --signal-* / --signal-rgb, or a component silently hard-codes the accent.

Signal layer Promote candidate

semantic accent · one switch, two themes

A semantic layer over the raw lime ramp. This is the single most valuable import: it’s what lets the whole system theme its accent from one place. Our product system currently uses raw --lime-400 / --cta-* for this role — I recommend promoting this group into the product tokens so the whole system can theme its accent.
